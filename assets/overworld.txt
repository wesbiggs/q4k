# The overworld in multicolour: the smooth-scrolling prototype's tiles
# (src/openworld.c, src/lib/smooth.s). tools/mkoverworld.py turns this into
# src/lib/ow_data.s.
#
# Every square of the map is a metatile of 2x2 characters, 16 by 16 pixels,
# drawn here as 16 rows of 8 double-width pixels:
#
#   .  background   $D021, shared by the whole screen
#   a  multicolour 1 $D022, shared
#   b  multicolour 2 $D023, shared
#   c  the character's own colour, from colour RAM: 0-7 only
#
# palette  bg mc1 mc2           the three shared colours
# offmap <tile>                 what lies beyond the map's edges
# fallback <tile>               what a tile with no art here is drawn as
# include <file>                another file's lines, here
# (This is one of the tile sets; the places' are *_tiles.txt, which use the
# same format. A tile's art may use - for the pixel of the tile it stands
# on: tile <name> <colour> on <tile>.)
# tile <name> <colour> [<colour> <colour> <colour>]
#     <name> is a tile of assets/tiles.txt; a second block with the same name
#     is a variant, picked by a hash of the square, so a field of grass is
#     not one stamp repeated. One colour is the whole metatile's; four are
#     its quadrants, top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right.
# shore <colour> [<headland colour>] [against <tile>...]
#                               the beaches, below: where water meets land
#                               (or only the tiles named)
# sprite <name> [bob]           8 double-width pixels by 16 rows (bob: and
#                                the same sunk a row, to idle between):
#     k  $D025 (shared)   s  $D026 (shared)   t  the sprite's own colour
#
# Water is animated: the generator lists its characters, and the engine
# rotates their rows a pixel at a time.

# Where water meets land: the top left quarter of a water square, with land
# to its left (west), above it (north), both, or only on the diagonal (an
# outside corner), 4 pixels by 8 rows each; the other three quarters are
# these mirrored. Each water quarter picks one from the three squares on its
# own corner (see smooth.s), so any coast is drawn in these. The fifth is a
# quarter of grass with sea both beside it and above it: its corner is
# rounded off with sand, so a headland does not end in a right angle.
#
# They have to meet each other without a seam, which comes down to: the
# diagonal's top row is the beside's top row, and its left column is the
# above's left column; both's bottom row is the beside's bottom row, and its
# right column is the above's right column. mkoverworld.py checks.
shore yellow

ccbb bbcb cccb bbbb c...
cccb bbbb ccbb bbbb ....
ccbb bbbb ccbb bbbb ..a.
cbbb bbbb ccbb bbbb .a.a
ccbb bbbb ccbb bbbb ....

# Where other ground meets open ground: these terrains fray at their edges
# instead of ending in a straight line. The masks are the top left quarter
# again, with open ground beside it, above it, both, or only on the diagonal;
# an x is cut back to the background (the grass's green). They are applied to
# each terrain's own art, every variant and every quarter, mirrored, so a new
# terrain only needs naming on a fringe line. Open ground is grass, and the
# places that stand on it. The same seams have to meet as the beaches'.
open grass town castle dungeon lair pass

# Where two of them meet, the lower frays toward the higher, which reads as
# a strip of grass between them: sand against the rest, swamp against forest
# and mountain, and a forest thins out at a mountain's foot.
also shore forest mountain swamp
also swamp forest mountain
also forest mountain

# Sand, mountains and swamp: a shallow, ragged edge (the forest's are

# two have the same ends, so they meet. A terrain drawn once is drawn twice
# (the same art) to have a second variant.

# The forest is trees, one to a quarter, so cutting pixels off its edge
# only nicks them and the next row stands in a straight line behind. Its
# edges are drawn instead: beside, above (or below), both, diagonal, and
# beside and above again for the second variant -- a tree drawn back from
# the open side, a bush in a corner, or just grass. Trees have a top, so
# these are not turned upside down: the first eight rows are the top left
# quarter (open ground to the west or north), the next eight the bottom
# left (west or south). Each is mirrored for the right-hand quarters, but
# for above and below, which look the same from either side.
